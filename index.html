How do I get this file?
MT5 (computer)
Toolbox at the bottom, History tab. Right-click, pick a period (e.g. last 3 months), then right-click, Report, save as HTML or Open XML. Drop that file here.
MT4 (computer)
Terminal, Account History tab. Right-click, All History, then right-click, Save as Detailed Report.
TradingView
Strategy Tester, List of trades, then the export (download) button, CSV. Note: that is a backtest, which usually looks better than live trading.
cTrader
History tab, then export to CSV or Excel.
Phone app or prop-firm dashboard
The MT4/MT5 phone apps cannot export history. Use the desktop app, or download your closed trades as CSV/Excel from your broker's or prop firm's dashboard. Any file with one row per closed trade and a Profit or P&L column works.
No file? Type your results instead

One profit/loss number per trade in account currency (losses negative), oldest first. Commas, spaces or new lines are fine. At least 20 trades.

New to this? The 30-second version

 
    
  
  • What a prop firm is: a company (FTMO, FundedNext, The5ers and others) that lets you trade its money and keeps part of the profit. To get the account you first pay for a test, called a challenge, usually $100 to $600.
    • 
  
  • The catch: the challenge has strict rules, for example reach +8% profit without ever losing more than 5% in one day or 10% in total. Break one rule once and the fee is gone.
    • 
  
  • Why most people fail: a few good weeks can be pure luck, and even a real edge can hit a losing streak that breaks the daily-loss rule.
    • 
  
  • What this page does: it takes the trades you already made and answers two questions before you pay: (1) could a coin flip have produced results like yours? (2) if you repeat the same kind of trades, how often would you pass, and which rule would most likely fail you?
    • 
 

 
Not for you if you have never traded: there is nothing to check yet. This is not financial advice and not a trading signal service.






 
What this does and does not tell you

 
    
  
  • It measures how reliable your recorded results look. It does not predict markets and does not say you will pass any challenge.
    • 
  
  • The pass rate assumes future trades behave like past ones and are independent. Markets change; streaks and regime shifts are not modelled.
    • 
  
  • Few trades means a wide range. A good-looking result on 30 trades is often indistinguishable from luck.
